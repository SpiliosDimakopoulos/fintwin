Simulation models of the firm promise conditional forecasts: how a revenue path turns into cash, and how likely a liquidity shortfall is under a stated shock. We test whether one such model, FinTwin, calibrated for each firm from point-in-time SEC XBRL filings, forecasts better than persistence. On a survivorship-free universe of 5,309 non-financial US-GAAP filers (2014-2024) and a frozen data snapshot, we compare an earlier specification with a corrected one under decision rules committed to the project repository before the runs, and list every later deviation. Given the realized revenue path, the corrections lower the median error of eight-quarter operating cash flow from 11.1 to 8.1 percentage points of trailing revenue, in every year, but leave a few explosive firm-years; a 16-quarter cash-flow margin remains more accurate (6.7 points). The model's response to revenue is 0.67 of the realized one, outside the pre-specified range of 0.8-1.25. Its departures from the margin are significant in squared-loss encompassing regressions, and adding a fifth of them to the margin lowers the winsorized mean absolute error by 3.3%; the gain is not significant under median loss and reverses on raw absolute loss. Its 90% predictive intervals cover 30% of outcomes, and its probability of a liquidity shortfall has a Brier skill of -0.20 against a logit on three opening ratios. Its health score ranks bankruptcies no better than its own formula without simulation. For this simulator, simulation mostly encodes persistence and does not deliver calibrated uncertainty.
