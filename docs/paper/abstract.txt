Simulation models of the firm promise conditional forecasts: how a revenue path turns into cash, and how likely a liquidity shortfall is under a stated shock. We test whether one such model, FinTwin, calibrated for each firm from point-in-time SEC XBRL filings, forecasts better than persistence. On a survivorship-free universe of 5,309 non-financial US-GAAP filers (2014-2024) and a frozen data snapshot, we compare an earlier specification with a corrected one under decision rules committed to a public repository before the runs. Given the realized revenue path, the corrections lower the median error of eight-quarter operating cash flow from 11.1 to 8.1 percentage points of trailing revenue, in every year, but leave a few explosive firm-years, and the corrected model is still less accurate than a 16-quarter cash-flow margin (6.7 points). Its response to revenue is 0.67 of the realized one, outside the pre-specified range of 0.8-1.25. Its departures from the margin are significant in squared-loss encompassing regressions; adding a fifth of them to the margin lowers the winsorized mean absolute error by 3.3%, a gain that disappears on raw absolute loss and that a model confidence set does not separate from simpler combinations. Its predictive distributions are far too narrow (a 90% interval covers 30% of outcomes), and its probability of a liquidity shortfall has a Brier skill of -0.20 against a logit on three opening ratios. Its health score ranks bankruptcies as well as, but no better than, its own formula without simulation. For this simulator, simulation mostly encodes persistence and does not deliver calibrated uncertainty.
